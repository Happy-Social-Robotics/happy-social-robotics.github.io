How robots signal

Six channels a robot uses to be understood.

Gaze

Where a robot looks directs attention, manages turns and shows interest.

VaryFixation length, direction, aversionMeasureObject recall, eye contact, conversation flow
Evidence and open question

Evidence. Reviews sort gaze work into three approaches: human-centered (how people respond), design-centered (change one feature, such as fixation time) and technology-centered (build the tools). The structure of the human eye makes gaze unusually easy to read.

Open question. How should gaze work with several people, and with robots whose eyes are screens or lights?

Admoni & Scassellati 2017

Face and expression

An expression must be both believable and readable. Those differ.

VaryExpression set, speed of changeMeasureRecognition accuracy, likeability
Evidence and open question

Evidence. Kismet separates believability (life-like) from readability (interpreted as intended). Its face shows emotional state, gives conversation cues, responds behaviorally and syncs with speech, moving smoothly through a space of arousal, valence and stance.

Open question. Do face-designed expressions carry over to robots with no face, such as a ball on wheels or a light bar?

MIT Kismet pages

Gesture

The cues that shape impressions of people shape impressions of robots.

VaryPresence, size, timing of a gestureMeasurePerceived trust, persuasiveness
Evidence and open question

Evidence. Breazeal says people use a robot’s nonverbal cues to judge how persuasive, likable and trustworthy it is. Treat this as a motivating account; read the underlying papers before citing it.

Open question. Which cues are universal, and which depend on culture?
