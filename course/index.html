Social Robotics Primer

Social robots are machines built to interact with people using the cues people already use: gaze, voice, gesture, timing, and movement through shared space. This primer takes you from first videos to a plan for your first workshop paper, in eight units.

Working definition

A social robot is a physically embodied machine whose usefulness depends on interaction with people, and which uses social cues to make that interaction work. The wording follows the "socially interactive robots" framing of Fong, Nourbakhsh and Dautenhahn (2003).

The path

The units are in order. Units 1 and 2 give you intuition. Units 3 to 7 build the concepts. Unit 8 turns them into a submission.

  1. Watch. Six short videos, from Stanford's Jackrabbot to MIT's Kismet and a hospice seal robot. About 45 minutes.
  2. Quiz. Twelve multiple-choice questions on what you just watched, with feedback on every answer.
  3. Foundations. What "social" means, a short history, and a simple model of how a social robot works.
  4. Types. Robots sorted by form, by application and by the role they play for people.
  5. Psychology. Anthropomorphism, the uncanny valley, mind perception, social responses and trust.
  6. Behavior. Gaze, expression, gesture, speech, space and adaptation: the channels a robot signals through.
  7. Methods and ethics. How studies are designed, which questionnaires are standard, and what you owe participants.
  8. Publish. Which venues suit a beginner, with current dates for 2027, and how to structure a short paper.

How each concept is written

Research labs present work with a question first, then evidence, then full citations. Stanford's Virtual Human Interaction Lab, for example, frames its work around the psychological and behavioral effects of virtual and augmented reality and lists its publications with complete references. This primer borrows that habit. Each concept block has four labels.

Claim
What the idea says, in one or two sentences.
Evidence
The study or review behind it, with citation.
Implication
What a robot designer or researcher should do differently.
Open question
Where a beginner can still contribute.

Every cited work is listed in full on the references page, with a DOI or link where one exists.

Dates and facts

Check before you rely on a date

Conference dates in Unit 8 were taken from the official conference websites on 29 October 2026. Deadlines move. Open the linked source before you plan around one.

Scope

This primer centers on human-robot interaction: how people perceive, respond to and are affected by robots that behave socially. Technical subfields such as perception, dialogue systems and motion planning appear only where they change the interaction. If your interest is mostly technical, use the references as a map and go deeper in the venues listed in Unit 8.
