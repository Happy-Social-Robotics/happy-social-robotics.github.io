Unit 4 · Part 1 of 4

Types of social robots

Sort a robot three ways: by form, by application, by role.

The story

Put five robots on one bench: a head that pulls faces, a seal you can stroke, a tutor for preschoolers, a ball that dodges pedestrians and a humanoid that gestures. Who would you hand each one to, and why?

1. By form

Anthropomorphic

Resembles a human body or face.

ExampleNexi, shown in Breazeal’s talk

Zoomorphic

Resembles an animal.

ExamplePARO, a baby harp seal

Caricatured

Exaggerated, simplified, creature-like features.

ExampleKismet; Tega

Functional

Shaped by its task. Social through behavior.

ExampleJackrabbot, a ball on wheels
ClaimForm sets expectations. The more humanlike the robot, the more social skill people expect.
Evidence

A very humanlike appearance can create expectations the robot cannot meet. Mori recommends a deliberately nonhuman design to keep affinity safe.

Bartneck et al. 2009Mori 2012
Implication

Match appearance to ability. A face that promises conversation should deliver it.

Open question

How do expectations shift as people see more robots?
