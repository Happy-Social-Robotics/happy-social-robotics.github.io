Types of social robots

Two ways to sort a robot: by what it looks like, and by where it works.

1. What does it look like?

  • AnthropomorphicHuman body or faceNexi
  • ZoomorphicAnimal shapePARO, a baby seal
  • CaricaturedExaggerated, creature-likeKismet, Tega
  • FunctionalShaped by its taskJackrabbot

Simplified drawings, not the named robots.

ClaimForm sets expectations. The more humanlike the robot, the more social skill people expect.
Evidence

A very humanlike appearance can create expectations the robot cannot meet. Mori recommends a deliberately nonhuman design to keep affinity safe.

Bartneck et al. 2009Mori 2012
Implication

Match appearance to ability. A face that promises conversation should deliver it.

Open question

How do expectations shift as people see more robots?
