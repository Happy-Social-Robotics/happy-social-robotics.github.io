Unit 7 of 8

Methods and ethics

A reviewer checks your question, design, measures and treatment of participants, in that order.

The story

A robot tutor is built, a study is run, and the results look great. Then a reviewer asks one question: who was secretly driving the robot? In a review of 54 Wizard of Oz experiments, only about 5% reported training the wizard.

1. From topic to question

An illustrative example, not from a paper.

FieldSocial robots in education
Population, settingPreschoolers hearing a story at school
BehaviorRobot gaze: toward the book or the child
OutcomeHow much of the story the child recalls
QuestionDoes gaze toward the book increase recall?

A good question has an independent variable, a dependent variable and a comparison. Units 5 and 6 say why you expect a difference.

2. Kinds of study

Lab experiment

Good forCausal claims about one variableWatch out forArtificial setting, small samples

Online video study

Good forMany participants, fast, cheapWatch out forWatching a robot differs from meeting one

Field or deployment

Good forReal use, real constraintsWatch out forMessy data. The 2026 “Failing Forward” workshop collects lessons

Interview or co-design

Good forNeeds, values, early ideasWatch out forNot generalizable; careful consent

Long-term study

Good forNovelty wearing off, relationshipsWatch out forCost, dropout, a robot that must keep working

Between-subjects

Each person sees one condition. No carry-over.

Within-subjects

Each person sees every condition. Fewer people, but counterbalance the order.

3. Wizard of Oz

54Wizard of Oz experiments reviewed, 2001–2011
~5%Reported training the wizard
<4%Measured wizard error
ClaimA hidden person operating the robot’s perception or speech is legitimate if reported properly.
Evidence

A review of 54 experiments proposed reporting guidelines in four parts: robot, user, wizard, general.

Riek 2012
Implication

Say who the wizard was, how they were trained, what choices they had, and whether participants thought the robot was autonomous.

Open question

How much do invisible wizard errors change results?

4. Measures

Godspeed

Five scales

  • Anthropomorphism
  • Animacy
  • Likeability
  • Perceived intelligence
  • Perceived safety
TipCheck reliability (Cronbach’s alpha); anthropomorphism and animacy overlap.

RoSAS

18 items, four studies

  • Warmth
  • Competence
  • Discomfort

Behavioral

What people do

  • Task performance
  • Recall
  • Time
  • Distance kept
  • Gaze to robot
  • Conversation length
Sample sizeSeveral items per concept let you check consistency. Use a power analysis when the expected difference is small. The education review averaged only 17–24 participants per data point.

5. Ethics

  1. ApprovalGet ethics-board approval first. Ask your supervisor which board applies.
  2. ConsentInformed consent. For children: parents or guardians, plus the child’s assent.
  3. DataDecide what you record, where it is stored and who can see it.

Six concerns in robot care for older people

  • Less human contact
  • Objectification, loss of control
  • Loss of privacy
  • Restricted liberty
  • Deception, infantilization
  • Responsibility when things go wrong

The worry is how robots are used, not that they are used.Sharkey & Sharkey 2012

Open questionThe PARO question from Unit 1: is it deceptive for a person with dementia to treat a robot as an animal? What does meaningful consent look like when participants cannot fully evaluate what a robot is?

6. Reporting checklist

Adapted from Riek’s four groups. Useful for any HRI study.

☐ Robot

Platform, behaviors, what it can and cannot do.

☐ Participants

Number, age, culture, robot experience, recruitment, what they were told.

☐ Operator

Any hidden control, training, error handling.

☐ General

Setting, scenario, ethics approval, limitations.

☐ Measures

Instrument, language, reliability, analysis, sample-size reasoning.
