Publishing your first paper

As a beginner you do not need a full conference paper. Short, early-stage venues exist in this field on purpose. This unit lays out the options for 2027, what is still open today, and how to structure a four-page paper.

Check each date at its source

Everything below was read from the official conference sites on 29 October 2026. Deadlines are usually 23:59 Anywhere on Earth (AoE), which is the last moment of the day in the UTC-12 time zone. Struck-through items have passed.

1. From idea to poster

  1. Question. One population, one setting, one behavior (Unit 7).
  2. Supervisor or co-author. Most venues expect your work to have been through ethics review and read by someone experienced.
  3. Pilot. A small study, an online video study, a design probe or a deployment story.
  4. Write 2 to 4 pages. Use the template the venue names (ACM "sigconf" for HRI).
  5. Submit and review. Some venues are double-blind: no author names in the file.
  6. Camera-ready. Add names, affiliations and the rights statement.
  7. Present. Short formats usually mean a poster, and often someone must attend in person.
  8. Extend. A workshop or late-breaking paper can grow into a full paper or journal article, if the venue was non-archival and you did not sign away the right.

The common pattern is a short paper, often 2 to 6 pages, with a theme narrow enough that a beginner can contribute a specific case or lesson. Many do not anonymize and some do not publish in an archive, so you can later extend the work.

5. Structure of a four-page paper

This outline is a rule of thumb, not a requirement. The page counts assume four pages excluding references.

SectionPagesWhat goes in it
Abstract0.1Question, method, main result, why it matters.
1 Introduction0.5The problem, the gap, and your contribution in one sentence.
2 Related work0.5Eight to twelve references, drawn from Units 5 and 6, that lead to your question.
3 Method1Robot, participants, procedure, measures, ethics (use the Unit 7 checklist).
4 Results1One main figure or table. Report sample size and reliability.
5 Discussion and limits0.75What you found, what you cannot claim, how it relates to the literature.
6 Future work0.25The next study, and what you would like to discuss at the workshop.

For a position paper with no data, replace Method and Results with a short argument and a proposed study design.

6. What reviewers look for

  • A clear, narrow question tied to prior work.
  • A method that can answer it, with enough detail to repeat.
  • Honest limits: small samples, one robot, one culture, one session.
  • Care for participants, especially children and older adults.
  • Writing that a researcher from a neighboring field can follow.

7. Practical points

  • Format. HRI asks for the ACM "sigconf" double-column template, not the SIGCHI one. Overleaf has an ACM template.
  • Archival status. Read each call. Late Breaking Reports at HRI go to a non-archival companion proceedings. Workshops differ: some publish on a website, some in proceedings. ACM became fully open access on 1 January 2026.
  • Double submission. Do not submit the same work to two venues that forbid it. Check each call.
  • Attending. LBR authors must present a poster in person. Plan travel and a visa early.
  • Funding. Pioneers may give some travel support, though not guaranteed. Student volunteering is another route. In 2026, a Meta-sponsored HRI special session offered travel support of up to US$1,500 for accepted authors of 1 to 2 page papers. Ask each year.
  • Lookalike conferences. A search for "human-robot interaction conference 2027" also returns listings from commercial organizers, for example "ICHRI" events that have no connection to the ACM/IEEE HRI conference. Check that a venue is sponsored by a professional society such as IEEE, ACM or the Robotics Society of Japan, and that its own society's site links to it.
