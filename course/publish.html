From idea to poster

  1. 1QuestionOne population, one setting, one behavior (Unit 7)
  2. 2Co-authorA supervisor; ethics review
  3. 3PilotSmall study, video study, design probe
  4. 4Write2–4 pages in the venue’s template (ACM sigconf)
  5. 5SubmitSome venues are double-blind
  6. 6Camera-readyNames, affiliations, rights
  7. 7PresentUsually a poster, often in person
  8. 8ExtendGrow it into a full paper if non-archival

The pattern: a short paper, 2–6 pages, on a theme narrow enough for a beginner to contribute one case or lesson. Many do not anonymize, and some do not publish in an archive, so you can extend the work later.

Structure of a four-page paper

A rule of thumb, not a requirement.

Abstract0.1 p
Introduction0.5 p
Related work0.5 p
Method1 p
Results1 p
Discussion0.75 p
Future0.25 p
Page 04 pages, excluding references

No data? Replace Method and Results with a short argument and a proposed study design.

What reviewers look for

  • A clear, narrow question tied to prior work
  • A method that can answer it, repeatable
  • Honest limits
  • Care for participants
  • Writing a neighboring field can follow

Practical points

Format

HRI uses the ACM “sigconf” double-column template, not SIGCHI. Overleaf has one.

Archival status

Read each call. HRI LBRs go to non-archival proceedings. Workshops differ. ACM has been fully open access since 1 January 2026.

Double submission

Do not submit the same work to two venues that forbid it.

Attending

LBR authors present a poster in person. Plan travel and visa early.

Funding

Pioneers may give some travel support. Student volunteering is another route. In 2026 a Meta-sponsored HRI session offered up to US$1,500 for accepted 1–2 page papers. Ask each year.

Lookalike conferencesSearching “human-robot interaction conference 2027” also returns commercial listings, such as “ICHRI”, unconnected to the ACM/IEEE conference. Check that the venue is sponsored by a society such as IEEE, ACM or the Robotics Society of Japan, and that the society’s own site links to it.
